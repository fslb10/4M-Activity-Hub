From your Unit 1.11 notes

Your lesson toolkit

These are concept reminders, not completed answers. Use the actual lesson and the article to explain your reasoning.

1. God is the source of moral truth

In Catholic thought, moral truth is grounded in God’s unchanging nature. It does not become true because someone believes it or votes for it. Unit 1.11, “Understanding Objective Moral Truth”.

2. Natural law, reason, and revelation

Natural law allows people to recognize basic moral principles through reason. Scripture (especially the Commandments and Jesus’ teachings), Sacred Tradition, and the Magisterium help clarify and apply them. Romans 2:14–15; CCC 1955–1956.

3. Conscience is more than a feeling

Conscience is a judgment that must be educated and directed toward what is genuinely good. Strong emotions, cultural approval, and sincerity do not automatically make an action morally right. Unit 1.11, “God, Natural Law, and the Source of Morality”.

4. Every person has inherent dignity

Every person is made in the image of God. The Catholic argument says human worth is not gained through independence, productivity, wealth, or health. Genesis 1:27; the class MAiD article, section 4.

5. Jesus deepens moral law through love

Jesus calls us beyond merely following rules toward love of God and neighbour. True compassion must include concrete care for people who suffer. Matthew 22:36–40; Unit 1.11, “Divine Law Revealed”.

6. Legal permission is a different question

Canadian law describes what the state permits. Catholic moral reasoning asks whether an action is consistent with moral truth as understood through God, reason, revelation, and the Church. The article uses MAiD to examine this difference.
